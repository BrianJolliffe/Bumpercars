## Recommended In-Ceiling Speakers

### 1. Polk Audio RC80i (Best Value) — ~$100/pair

- **Driver:** 8" dynamic balance mineral/polymer composite cone woofer + 1" silk/polymer dome tweeter
- **Sensitivity:** 89 dB
- **Power handling:** 100W continuous
- **Impedance:** 6-8 ohms
- **Cut-out size:** ~10.5" round
- **Mounting:** Rotating cam dog-ear clamps grip drywall securely
- **Grille:** Paintable white grille
- **Sold as:** Pair
- **Why it's good:** Best bang-for-buck. The RC80i has a moisture-resistant design suitable for kitchens/bathrooms, a wall-proximity switch for placement flexibility, and a glass-composite baffle that reduces resonance. Spring-loaded push terminals make wiring fast. Consistently rated as the top budget pick across multiple review sites.
- **Watch out for:** Can sound slightly thin at the highs and muddy at the lows compared to pricier options.

### 2. Yamaha NS-IC800 (Best Mid-Range) — ~$150–180/pair

- **Driver:** 8" blue polypropylene mica cone woofer + fluid-cooled soft-dome swivel tweeter (coaxial)
- **Sensitivity:** 90 dB
- **Power handling:** 140W max
- **Impedance:** 8 ohms
- **Frequency response:** 50 Hz – 28 kHz
- **Cut-out size:** ~10.25" round
- **Mounting:** Large mounting clamp with non-slip tread for firm grip
- **Grille:** Paintable magnetic grille (easy snap-on/off)
- **Sold as:** Pair
- **Why it's good:** The swivel tweeter lets you aim high frequencies toward the listening area — a big advantage for ceiling-mounted speakers. Magnetic grilles make install/maintenance effortless. Excellent bass response for an in-ceiling (down to 50 Hz). Widely considered the benchmark in its price range.
- **Watch out for:** Needs a slightly larger ceiling cutout than 6.5" models.

### 3. Klipsch CDT-5650-C II (Best Premium) — ~$185/each

- **Driver:** 6.5" pivoting ceramic/metallic composite woofer + 1" titanium tweeter (coaxial, horn-loaded)
- **Sensitivity:** 93 dB (very efficient — needs less power)
- **Power handling:** 75W continuous
- **Impedance:** 8 ohms
- **Cut-out size:** ~8.5" round (fits in tighter spaces)
- **Mounting:** Dog-ear clamp system
- **Grille:** Paintable magnetic grille
- **Sold as:** Single (buy 2)
- **Why it's good:** 360-degree rotating woofer and aimable tweeter let you dial in the sound. Built-in treble and mid-bass attenuation switches help compensate for room shape. The horn-loaded tweeter is a Klipsch hallmark — punchy, detailed, and very efficient. Near-audiophile quality.
- **Watch out for:** Sold individually so a pair costs ~$370. Some listeners find Klipsch a bit bright.

## Installation Tips for a Single-Day Install

1. **Plan speaker placement first** — Ideally 6–8 feet apart, centered on the primary listening area. Avoid placing directly above seating if possible (slightly offset sounds more natural).
2. **Check for obstructions** — Use a stud finder to confirm no joists, ductwork, or wiring are in the way before cutting.
3. **Use the included template** — All three speaker models include a cut-out template. Trace it, then cut with a drywall saw.
4. **Run speaker wire beforehand** — 16-gauge (for runs under 50 ft) or 14-gauge (for longer runs) CL2-rated in-wall speaker wire. Fish the wire from the receiver location to each speaker cutout.
5. **Connect and mount** — Strip wire ends, connect to spring/push terminals, push the speaker into the cutout, and tighten the mounting clamps until snug.
6. **Snap on the grille** — Magnetic grilles (Yamaha/Klipsch) just pop on. The Polk uses a friction-fit grille.
7. **Run room calibration** — Use the receiver's included microphone to auto-calibrate (YPAO for Yamaha, Audyssey for Denon).

**Realistic timeline:** For 2 speakers with pre-planned wire routes, expect 2–4 hours including wire fishing, cutting holes, mounting, and calibration.
